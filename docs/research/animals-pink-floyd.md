## How it was made

- **Studio.** In 1975 the band bought a three-storey block of church halls at 35 Britannia Row, Islington, and converted it into their own studio and storage space. *Animals* was the first album made there. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- **Old material.** Two songs the band played live in 1974 and considered for *Wish You Were Here* ("Raving and Drooling" and "You've Got to Be Crazy") were reworked into "Sheep" and "Dogs". Only "Pigs (Three Different Ones)" and the two "Pigs on the Wing" parts were new. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)), [Louder](https://loudersound.com/features/raving-and-drooling-how-pink-floyd-made-animals))
- **Pigs on the Wing.** It is a short love song bookending the album and reflects Waters' relationship with his then-wife. Snowy White recorded a solo for the 8-track version after Waters and Mason accidentally erased one of Gilmour's solos; it is not on the vinyl. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- **"Pigs" recording.** "Pigs (Three Different Ones)" was recorded April to May 1976. Gilmour plays the solo through a talk box to imitate pig sounds, the band's first use of one. Waters plays rhythm guitar and Gilmour plays bass with a pick. ([Wikipedia: Pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_(Three_Different_Ones)))
- **Engineer's account.** Brian Humphries says he kept the studio running when the band went on holiday, that Waters and Mason asked for dog and sheep sound effects while he worked on other things, and that Gilmour's overdubbed drums on "Dogs" were wiped without his knowledge and had to be re-recorded. For "Sheep" he told Wright "Rick, just do your thing" and used the first of about four takes. ([Louder](https://loudersound.com/features/raving-and-drooling-how-pink-floyd-made-animals), secondary, quoting a primary participant)

## Concept and meaning

- **Orwell.** The album is loosely based on George Orwell's *Animal Farm*. Society is sorted into predatory **dogs** (ruthless businessmen), despotic **pigs** (the powerful and the moralising) and mindless, unquestioning **sheep**. Unlike Orwell's attack on Stalinism, it is aimed at capitalism and 1970s Britain, and at the end the sheep rise up against the dogs. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)), [Far Out](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/pink-floyd-george-orwell-animals/), [Open Culture](https://www.openculture.com/2017/04/pink-floyd-adapts-orwells-animal-farm-into-its-1977-concept-album-animals.html))
- **Waters on it.** He described it as "a sketch, a sort of cartoon sketch of how I saw the way society was organized" (as quoted in web search summaries; the original interview was not located).
- **"Pigs (Three Different Ones)".** According to Waters, the three verses target businessmen in general, Margaret Thatcher (then Leader of the Opposition) and morality campaigner Mary Whitehouse. In 1992 Waters told Jim Ladd the "Whitehouse" was not about the White House. ([Wikipedia: Pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_(Three_Different_Ones)))
- **Punk.** Mason, 2019: "I suppose I always saw Animals as more of a punk album." ([Louder](https://loudersound.com/features/raving-and-drooling-how-pink-floyd-made-animals)). Mason also produced The Damned's *Music for Pleasure* at Britannia Row in 1977. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- **Wright.** In 1978 he told *Melody Maker*'s Karl Dallas that "Animals was certainly mostly Roger's ideas, and Dave wrote the music for Dogs", and that he "didn't contribute anything". ([Louder](https://loudersound.com/features/raving-and-drooling-how-pink-floyd-made-animals))

## Band tension

- It was the first Pink Floyd album where Richard Wright had no writing credit. He left the band two years after release (reported as being fired). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- Humphries said "I think Nick and Roger had already made their mind up" that Wright "wasn't going to be around for the next album". ([Louder](https://loudersound.com/features/raving-and-drooling-how-pink-floyd-made-animals))
- The 1977 *In the Flesh* tour began in Dortmund on release day. Wikipedia reports Waters arriving and leaving venues alone, and Wright once flying home threatening to quit. In Montreal in July 1977 Waters spat at a fan, an incident Wikipedia says helped inspire *The Wall* (1979). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))

## Cover art and the pig

- Roger Waters conceived the cover after Hipgnosis's own ideas were rejected. He cycled around London with a camera and settled on Battersea Power Station, which he called "doomy, inhuman". Hipgnosis (Storm Thorgerson and Aubrey Powell) produced it. ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/all-hell-broke-loose-the-chaotic-penny-pinching-story-behind-pink-floyds-animals-album-cover-photoshoot), citing Blake's *Comfortably Numb*)
- An inflatable pig nicknamed "Algie" was floated at the power station. Manager Steve O'Rourke hired a marksman to shoot it down if it escaped, but for only the first day. Waters on the pig: "It's a symbol of hope". ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/all-hell-broke-loose-the-chaotic-penny-pinching-story-behind-pink-floyds-animals-album-cover-photoshoot))
- On the second day the wind pulled the pig free with no marksman present. Powell: "All hell broke loose." The pig was reported by the RAF and Heathrow air traffic control, reached about 18,000 feet, and landed in a field in Godmersham, Kent, where the farmer was furious because it had scared his cows. It was recovered for the third day. ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/all-hell-broke-loose-the-chaotic-penny-pinching-story-behind-pink-floyds-animals-album-cover-photoshoot))
- Wikipedia says the final image used a professional photograph by Howard Bartrop. The picture labels show a dog, a pig and a sheep, and Mason's handwriting is used as a typeface. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- Waters kept the rights to the pig after leaving the band. It broke loose again at a 2008 Coachella show, and a replica flew over Battersea in 2011 for the reissue campaign. (web search summaries; not confirmed from a fetched page)

## Reception and legacy

- **At release.** Reviews were mixed. Rolling Stone's Frank Rose was negative; Robert Christgau gave a B+; NME and Melody Maker were positive. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- **Since.** Retrospective ratings are high (Wikipedia lists Pitchfork 10/10, PopMatters 9/10) and many consider it among the band's best. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- **Charts.** UK No. 2 and US No. 3 originally. Pink Floyd had 108 weeks on the 1977 UK Albums Chart, more than ABBA. The 2022 reissue reached No. 21 on the US Billboard 200. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- **Certifications (selected).** US 4× Platinum (31 January 1995); UK Platinum; Canada 2× Platinum; Germany Platinum; France Platinum. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)); should be confirmed against RIAA and BPI.)

## Reissues

- CD in 1985 (UK) and 1987 (US); remastered CD in 1994; remastered vinyl in 1997. Included in the *Shine On* box set (1992), *Oh, By The Way* (2007) and *Why Pink Floyd...?* (2011).
- New mix released 16 September 2022 (vinyl, CD, Blu-ray), deluxe edition 7 October 2022, plus a multichannel SACD. A 2018 remix of "Dogs" came out as a digital single on 22 July 2022.
- Waters said in 2020 he proposed a remix by James Guthrie but Gilmour and Mason rejected it; in June 2021 he announced a new mix.
- Wikipedia lists a 50th-anniversary edition, *Animals 50*, for 22 January 2027. This is a future date and should be checked against an official announcement.
