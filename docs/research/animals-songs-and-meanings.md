# Pink Floyd, *Animals*: song meanings

Companion to [animals-pink-floyd.md](animals-pink-floyd.md). Gathered 2026-11-21 from web search and page fetches. Raw research, not approved content.

**Ground rules**
- **No lyrics are reproduced.** Songs are described in paraphrase with at most a few words quoted. This matches the app's rule that lyric text never goes in the repo or database; lyrics would come from a licensed provider at runtime.
- Every reading is labelled by who said it, using the app's claim types:
  - **Artist intent:** a band member speaking (needs a primary source to be approved).
  - **Fact:** documented history of the song.
  - **Interpretation:** a critic's or fan's reading, credited to them.
- Almost all material is second-hand (Wikipedia, music press, Songfacts). Where Songfacts is the source, user comments are separated from editorial text.

## The album's overall idea

- **Artist intent.** Waters, 2017 *Uncut* interview: "It's a sketch, a sort of cartoon sketch of how I saw the way society was organized." (via [Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/pink-floyd/dogs); the original interview was not read)
- **Fact.** Loosely modelled on Orwell's *Animal Farm*: **dogs** are ruthless businessmen, **pigs** are the powerful and the moralising, **sheep** are the passive public. Unlike Orwell's Stalinism, the target is capitalism and 1970s Britain. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animals_(Pink_Floyd_album)))
- **Artist intent (Waters on "Pigs on the Wing").** He said the song was "very necessary", and that without it "the album would have just been a kind of scream, you know, of rage." (via [Far Out](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/song-roger-waters-said-was-necessary-to-animals/); date and outlet not stated)

## 1. Pigs on the Wing (Part One) and (Part Two)

**What it is.** Two short acoustic pieces (1:24 each) that open and close the album. Only Waters' strummed guitar and voice. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_on_the_Wing))

**What it means**
- **Artist intent / fact.** It is a love song. Nick Mason said, and Waters confirmed, that it was written for Waters' then-partner Carolyne Christie, as someone who could hold her own in an argument with him. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_on_the_Wing); Mason's statement originally from his book, which was not read)
- **Artist intent (later).** In 2017, Waters told *Mojo* it "maybe" was about one person, but that he now sees it as more general: the need for care and empathy between people. ([Far Out](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/song-roger-waters-said-was-necessary-to-animals/), second-hand)
- **Interpretation.** Brice Ezell, *Consequence of Sound*, 2017: Part One is a brief acoustic frame whose major key contrasts with the album's menacing riffs; Part Two reminds us that people find ways to stick together under a harsh, capitalist order. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_on_the_Wing))
- **Role on the album.** Hope and warmth as a counterweight to the three angry songs (see Waters' "scream of rage" remark above).

**History**
- The 8-track version is 3:26 and joins the two parts with an electric guitar solo by Snowy White, with Wright on Hammond organ. The vinyl has no solo. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_on_the_Wing))
- Released as a promotional single on 21 January 1977 (Wikipedia). The "Sheep" page also says it was a promo single with "Pigs on the Wing" as the A-side.

**Conflicts:** her name is spelled "Carolyne" (Wikipedia) and "Caroline" (Far Out). Far Out says they divorced in 1992; not confirmed elsewhere.

## 2. Dogs (17:04)

**What it is.** The album's longest track, written by Gilmour and Waters (Waters wrote the lyrics). Gilmour sings the first half and Waters the second. It grew from "You've Got to Be Crazy", played live in 1974; Waters reworked the words, lowered the key so both could sing it, and cut many words that were too many to sing. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogs_(Pink_Floyd_song)))

**What it is about (fact, from Wikipedia's summary of the lyrics)**
1. A charming, respectable businessman who preys on those who trust him.
2. As he ages his life empties out and he sinks under a metaphorical weight, the "stone".
3. Closing section: office workers compared to dogs, trained, controlled and rewarded, while neglecting their families. It ends on a run of lines that each begin with "who was".

**Artist intent**
- Waters said one line in the last section was personal, because he had been separated from his family in infancy. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogs_(Pink_Floyd_song)))
- Waters denied that Allen Ginsberg's "Howl" influenced the repeated "who was" structure, in an interview quoted in Phil Rose's book. (via Wikipedia; book not read)
- Waters said the album's closing section is a bitter reaction against authoritarianism ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/pink-floyd/dogs), attributed to a 2017 *Uncut* interview).

**Interpretation (all unverified, credited as given)**
- Most commentary reads it as a critique of cutthroat business culture. ([Songfacts](https://www.songfacts.com/facts/pink-floyd/dogs); fan and aggregator sites in the search results)
- Readers' guesses at the "stone": a bag weighted with a stone used to drown surplus puppies (Songfacts commenter "Danny"); depression (commenter "Tim"). Neither is sourced. *No band statement explaining the stone was found.*
- Other reader proposals include the Cold War and a dialogue between a person and their alter ego. Unsourced.
- A claim that Waters said "pigs are politicians, dogs are the businessmen who support them, sheep are the masses" is reported by an anonymous Songfacts commenter with no source, so treat it as unverified.

**Making of**
- Opens on acoustic guitar and Farfisa organ; the middle section in 6/4 uses layered synthesisers and dog barks processed through a vocoder, with Gilmour's "stone" echoing over it. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogs_(Pink_Floyd_song)))
- The earlier 1974 versions were faster and in E minor before the band tuned down to D. A 2014 bootleg, *The Extraction Tapes*, has a roughly 14-minute early studio version.
- Engineer Brian Humphries recalled Gilmour's overdubbed drums being wiped and re-recorded. ([Louder](https://loudersound.com/features/raving-and-drooling-how-pink-floyd-made-animals)). Wikipedia's *Dogs* page does not describe a re-record.
- Gilmour (*Guitar World*, February 1993), on the harmonies: "Three-part, in some cases; it's two-part in the melody sections." (via Songfacts)

**Disputed:** how the dog sounds were made. Songfacts commenters variously say vocoder-processed recordings, a Hammond organ through a synth, or fully synthesised. Wikipedia says vocoder-processed barks.

## 3. Pigs (Three Different Ones) (11:28)

**What it is.** A Waters song about the powerful. Recorded April to May 1976 at Britannia Row. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_(Three_Different_Ones)))

**What it means**
- **Artist intent.** According to Waters, the three verses target businessmen in general, Margaret Thatcher (then Leader of the Opposition) and the morality campaigner Mary Whitehouse. In 1992 he told Jim Ladd the "Whitehouse" was not the White House, after Ladd suggested the last verse referred to Gerald Ford. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_(Three_Different_Ones)); the interview itself was not read)
- **Fact.** The pigs are the people at the top who hold wealth and power and push others to compete ruthlessly to keep it.

**Making of**
- Gilmour plays the solo through a talk box to imitate pig sounds, the band's first use of a talk box. He plays bass with a pick, with two short syncopated bass solos; Waters plays rhythm guitar. (Wikipedia)

**Live life**
- Live versions ran about 17 to 20 minutes. On the 1977 tour Waters shouted a different number at each show, reportedly so bootleg recordings could be identified (attributed to Mason via an agent).
- Waters revived it in 2016 with anti-Donald Trump imagery. During the 2017 *Us + Them* tour some fans booed or walked out, and Waters said he was "slightly surprised" that anyone who followed his work for 50 years would react that way. He also said he lost sponsors, including American Express, over the imagery. (Wikipedia)

**Conflict:** Wikipedia gives the single edit as 4:03 in the infobox but about 4:05 in the text. It was a Brazilian promo single, released 23 January 1977.

## 4. Sheep (10:20)

**What it is.** Written and sung by Waters, and the album's counterpart to "Dogs". It began as "Raving and Drooling" in 1974. Waters moved it to *Animals* because he felt it did not fit *Wish You Were Here*'s theme of absence. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_(Pink_Floyd_song)))

**What it means**
- **Fact (Wikipedia's reading, under a "needs more citations" tag).** The sheep are the working class, who follow the dogs and pigs without questioning their exploitation until they wake up and rebel. In this album's version, unlike Orwell's, the sheep overpower the dogs.
- **Fact.** The middle section is a parody of Psalm 23, spoken (processed through a vocoder, recorded by Mason).
- **Interpretation / context.** Wikipedia ties the song to the 1976 Notting Hill Carnival riots and the Troubles in Northern Ireland, and says Waters expected similar unrest, such as the Brixton and Toxteth riots of 1981. These links are Wikipedia's framing; no direct Waters statement was found.
- **Artist intent (2020, Waters, via Louder):** "We don't rebel against Trump, we elect him", presented as the ultimate act for the *Sheep*. This is a much later, political comment, not a statement of what the song meant in 1977.

**Making of**
- Recorded April, May and July 1976. Waters moved from bass to rhythm guitar and Gilmour overdubbed the bass. Wright plays the Rhodes piano introduction over sheep noises. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_(Pink_Floyd_song)))
- Engineer Brian Humphries said he told Wright "Rick, just do your thing" and used the first of about four takes for the opening. ([Louder](https://loudersound.com/features/raving-and-drooling-how-pink-floyd-made-animals))
- The 1974 live version used a cut-up recording of BBC DJ Jimmy Young, meant to evoke a man "raving and drooling". The 1974 Wembley recording was released in 2011 in the *Wish You Were Here* Experience and Immersion editions.
- The *Echoes* compilation includes a 9:46 edit that fades into "Sorrow".
- Ian Peel (*The Guardian*) noted the bassline and effects resemble the *Doctor Who* theme.

**Takeaway.** Song meaning is where this app's sourcing rules do the most work. The history of how each song was made is well documented. What the songs mean mostly rests on a few Waters quotes, and each of those is currently second-hand. Turning them into approvable claims means retrieving the original interviews, and the Capital Radio transcript is the first place to try.
